Actuator

A single Rust binary that lets AI agents see a phone screen and act on it: an MCP server that drives Android emulators, iOS simulators and cloud device farms from screenshots, not element trees. It finds a target by sight and taps by coordinate, so it reaches apps other tools can't, even ones drawn entirely on a canvas. On iOS, touch goes in through IndigoHID mach ports, which I reverse-engineered, and a headless XCUITest runner of my own replaces WebDriverAgent. Listed in the official MCP registry in March 2026, launched publicly in June 2026.

Graspline

An engineering graphics (descriptive geometry) solver for first-year engineering students and their teachers. A student types a textbook question and Graspline draws the whole construction step by step, checking every measurement numerically, for projections of points, lines, planes and solids. A small fine-tuned model reads the question and a deterministic geometry engine draws and verifies it, which keeps running costs near zero. Teachers can generate practice sets with answer keys.

Marram

A local-first wellness app for women in perimenopause and menopause, in development. It is built to read how she uses her phone, never what she writes (touch dynamics, keystroke timing, usage and sleep rhythm), against her own baseline, and to offer a calming exercise generated fresh each time: paced breathing, a soft-body sensory canvas, or grounding. A personal Random Forest, written in pure Dart, trains on the phone, so her data is designed to stay there. Its program follows menopause-specific CBT, 12 self-paced weeks and 35 sessions backed by 37 citations. Wellness, not diagnosis. Flutter, for iOS and Android.

Analytics

Years of watching product decisions rest on misread analytics led to this. A product analytics SDK for mobile and web apps that captures events by itself, so developers write no tracking code. It watches screen changes, taps, network calls and crashes and turns them into named business events. Personal data it recognises is redacted or sealed on the device before upload. Multi-tenant backend: Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects for ingestion, Supabase Postgres with row-level security for tenants and keys, and a ClickHouse event store on a self-hosted server.

Haptura

Built for Meta's Global AI Developer Hackathon, Startup track. Before a phone agent taps, Haptura asks Android's input system which app owns the window under that point and refuses the tap if it isn't the task's app. Meta's model, Muse, proposes every action and the gate decides. When an attack dialog appeared mid-task, the attacker's app received touches in 9 of 9 runs for both Muse Spark and Muse Wonder without the gate, and in none with it, and all 18 gated runs still finished the task. On a real Galaxy S26 it refused 21 of 21 taps aimed at attack dialogs.

Entorhinal

Built for the Nebius × NVIDIA Global AI Hackathon, Personal AI track. A memory for an AI assistant that notices when something new makes an old fact untrue, and checks a question against it before answering. On 12 scenarios from the public STALE benchmark it got 18 of 36 answers right, against 6 of 36 for the same Nemotron model reading the whole history. It refused 6 of 12 questions resting on a fact that was no longer true, where that model refused none.
